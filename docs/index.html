    What we claim, and what we don't

    We test whether a model trained on 2012–2022 forecasts 20-day volatility on 2023–2024 more accurately than (a) each asset's historical average volatility, (b) the most recent 20-day volatility, and (c) the RiskMetrics EWMA estimate.

    We do not predict returns or price direction, and nothing here is investment advice. Results apply only to these four ETFs and this period.

      Limitations

      • Regime dependence. The model learned that volatility tends to fall back to normal. That helped in the calm 2023–2024 test period but hurt in 2022, when volatility stayed high for months; EWMA beat it on validation. One test period of about 22 months cannot show that the advantage lasts.
      • One-day shocks. A VIX spike that fades within days (for example 5 August 2024) leads to large over-predictions, because the model reads it like the start of a crisis.
      • Sudden stress without warning. When volatility jumps with no prior signal (the July 2024 tech rotation), every forecast under-predicts.
      • Overlapping targets. Consecutive days share 19 of their 20 target days, so there are far fewer independent observations than rows. That is why confidence intervals use a moving-block bootstrap.
      • Noisy target. Realised volatility from 20 daily returns is itself a noisy estimate of true volatility, so even a perfect model would show error.
      • Small, liquid universe. Four US-listed ETFs and one horizon only. Results may not carry over to single stocks, other markets or other horizons.
      • Single data vendor. Prices come from Yahoo Finance and were not cross-checked against another source.

      Preprocessing choices and why

      • Log returns: they add up over time; volatility is the standard deviation of daily returns × √252.
      • Log of the target and of volatility features: volatility is positive and right-skewed and errors grow with its level. In logs the relation is close to linear (HAR-style). The back-transform uses Duan's smearing factor, estimated on training data.
      • Standardisation inside a Pipeline: the scaler is refit on each fold's training rows only, so validation and test statistics never leak into training.
      • 20-day embargo: a row dated t uses returns up to t+20. Rows whose target crosses a split boundary are dropped and each CV fold leaves a 20-day gap.
      • Date-based folds: all assets of one day stay on the same side of a split.
      • Regimes from training data: calm / normal / stressed cut-offs are VIX terciles of the training years, so the test set does not define them.
